# Research Prompt: channel-advisor

**For:** `cc-concept:channel-advisor`
**Current knowledge base:** `plugins/cc-concept/skills/_shared/campaign-frameworks.md`'s
RACE section (Reach, Act, Convert, Engage), used here as the sole framework
for channel-mix recommendations by budget and audience.
**Relationship to other prompts:** `campaign-concept.md` in this folder covers
RACE's general standing as part of the three-framework composite. This prompt
goes deeper on the part `channel-advisor` actually leans on: choosing and
sequencing channels for a given budget and audience.

Paste the prompt below into a deep-research AI tool (Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini,
Perplexity, or similar). Save the response as Markdown, e.g. to
`docs/research/channel-advisor.md`, then use it to refine
`campaign-frameworks.md`'s RACE section.

---

```
─────────────────────────────────────────────
Research prompt: marketing channel mix and sequencing decisions
─────────────────────────────────────────────
You are a research analyst investigating evidence-based practice for
recommending a marketing channel mix and sequence for a given goal, target
audience, and budget, for use in an AI marketing-strategy tool built around
the RACE framework (Reach, Act, Convert, Engage).

Cover, with headers separating each area:

1. Current research or well-evidenced industry benchmarks on channel
   effectiveness by audience type (B2B vs. B2C, by company size for B2B) and
   by funnel stage — which channels reliably outperform for reach vs.
   conversion vs. retention, and how much this varies by industry.
2. Budget-allocation research or established practitioner heuristics for
   channel mix (e.g. the 70-20-10 marketing budget rule, media mix modeling
   basics) — what's actually evidence-backed versus rule-of-thumb.
3. Research on channel sequencing specifically — does the order channels are
   introduced in a customer journey measurably affect outcomes (e.g.
   multi-touch attribution research, exposure-sequence effects), and what
   does that imply for how a RACE-based recommendation should be sequenced?
4. Emerging or currently underrated channels worth flagging as options
   (per recent industry research/surveys) that a static framework like RACE,
   designed pre-dating them, might under-recommend.
5. Common channel-mix mistakes documented in case studies or practitioner
   research (e.g. over-concentration risk, channel cannibalization,
   attribution blind spots) that a recommendation tool should actively guard
   against.

Require credible, verifiable sources throughout, with in-text citations —
prioritize peer-reviewed marketing research, recognized industry benchmark
reports (e.g. HubSpot State of Marketing, Gartner), and established
practitioner publications over generic marketing-blog content. Provide a
formatted source list in APA style at the end. Respond in Markdown.
─────────────────────────────────────────────
```
