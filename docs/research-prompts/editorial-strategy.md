# Research Prompt: editorial-strategy

**For:** `cc-concept:editorial-strategy`
**Current knowledge base:** `plugins/cc-concept/skills/_shared/campaign-frameworks.md`'s
Message House section, used here as the sole framework for content pillars
and topic clusters.
**Relationship to other prompts:** `campaign-concept.md` in this folder covers
Message House's general standing as part of the three-framework composite.
This prompt goes deeper on the part `editorial-strategy` actually leans on:
turning a message architecture into content pillars, topic clusters, a
content mix, and a publishing cadence.

Paste the prompt below into a deep-research AI tool (Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini,
Perplexity, or similar). Save the response as Markdown, e.g. to
`docs/research/editorial-strategy.md`, then use it to refine
`campaign-frameworks.md`'s Message House section.

---

```
─────────────────────────────────────────────
Research prompt: content strategy — pillars, topic clusters, and cadence
─────────────────────────────────────────────
You are a research analyst investigating evidence-based practice for
editorial/content strategy — organizing content into pillars and topic
clusters, choosing a content-type mix, and setting a publishing cadence — for
use in an AI marketing-strategy tool built around a Message-House-style
message architecture (one core message, several supporting pillars).

Cover, with headers separating each area:

1. The pillar-and-cluster content model (topic clusters / content pillars)
   — origin (this term is closely associated with HubSpot's SEO-driven
   pillar-cluster model), evidence for its effectiveness, and how it relates
   to or differs from a Message-House-style pillar structure originally
   designed for PR/messaging rather than content organization.
2. Research or well-evidenced industry data on content-type mix by funnel
   stage (TOFU/MOFU/BOFU) and by content goal (thought leadership, lead
   generation, retention) — what actually correlates with better outcomes at
   each stage, versus commonly repeated but unverified claims.
3. Publishing cadence research — is there evidence for an optimal
   posting/publishing frequency by content type and channel, or does
   available research suggest cadence is secondary to consistency and
   quality (and if so, what does the evidence say about the trade-off)?
4. How mature organizations validate a content strategy before committing to
   it (e.g. content audits, audience research methods, message testing) —
   what would meaningfully improve on a strategy generated purely from
   stated business goals and audience without that validation step.
5. Any research on how AI-assisted content strategy generation should be
   scoped/caveated given current limitations (e.g. risk of generic pillar
   recommendations that don't reflect genuine competitive differentiation).

Require credible, verifiable sources throughout, with in-text citations —
prioritize peer-reviewed marketing/communication research and recognized
industry benchmark reports over generic content-marketing-blog content.
Provide a formatted source list in APA style at the end. Respond in
Markdown.
─────────────────────────────────────────────
```
