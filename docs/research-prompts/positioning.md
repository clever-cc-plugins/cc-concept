# Research Prompt: positioning

**For:** `cc-concept:positioning`
**Current knowledge base:** `plugins/cc-concept/skills/_shared/positioning-frameworks.md` —
April Dunford's Obviously Awesome process, Geoffrey Moore's positioning
statement template, the Value Proposition Canvas, and the Perceptual Map,
each with a "best for / avoid" selection guide. Applied from general
knowledge; no dedicated research doc yet, unlike `audience-personas` and
`competitive-research` which this skill's Step 2 can delegate to.
**Why research helps:** these four frameworks are all widely taught but have
different evidence pedigrees (some are structured practitioner methodology,
others closer to academic marketing theory) — worth knowing which is which.

Paste the prompt below into a deep-research AI tool (Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini,
Perplexity, or similar). Save the response as Markdown, e.g. to
`docs/research/positioning.md`, then use it to refine
`positioning-frameworks.md`.

---

```
─────────────────────────────────────────────
Research prompt: product/brand positioning frameworks
─────────────────────────────────────────────
You are a research analyst investigating the evidence base and current
practitioner standing of four product/brand positioning frameworks, for use
in an AI marketing-strategy tool that selects among them based on the
positioning challenge at hand.

Cover, with headers separating each area:

1. April Dunford's "Obviously Awesome" positioning process (competitive
   alternatives → unique attributes → value → best-fit segment → market
   frame) — origin, current practitioner standing (this is a relatively
   recent, practitioner-originated framework), and any published evidence
   or case-study validation of its effectiveness versus being popular but
   informally validated.
2. Geoffrey Moore's positioning statement template ("For [target] who
   [need], [product] is a [category] that [benefit]. Unlike [alternative],
   we [differentiator].") — origin (Crossing the Chasm), how it's held up
   as marketing practice has evolved, and known limitations for
   multi-segment or highly technical products.
3. The Value Proposition Canvas (Strategyzer / Osterwalder) — evidence base,
   how it relates to and differs from Business Model Canvas methodology it's
   often paired with, and research on its actual effectiveness for
   surfacing product-market-fit gaps versus being a facilitation tool with
   softer validation.
4. Perceptual mapping as a positioning tool — academic marketing-research
   standing (this has the longest academic history of the four, going back
   to classical marketing-science literature on multidimensional scaling),
   and current best practice for choosing which two axes to map.
5. Any current, well-evidenced research on how to choose among positioning
   frameworks for a given situation — is there research-backed guidance
   beyond practitioner "best for / avoid" heuristics, and are there
   well-evidenced frameworks not covered here (e.g. Jobs-to-be-Done-based
   positioning, category design) worth adding to the selection set?

Require credible, verifiable sources throughout, with in-text citations —
prioritize peer-reviewed marketing-science research and well-established
practitioner sources (books, published frameworks with documented origin)
over generic marketing-blog content. Be explicit about which frameworks have
academic/empirical backing versus practitioner-methodology status only.
Provide a formatted source list in APA style at the end. Respond in
Markdown.
─────────────────────────────────────────────
```
